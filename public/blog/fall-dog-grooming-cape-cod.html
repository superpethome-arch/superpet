Fall Dog Grooming on Cape Cod: How to Prepare Your Dog's Coat for Winter

September 23, 2026 · 7 min read · Super Pet Grooming Salon

Quick answer: In the fall, focus on removing the loose summer undercoat, checking for ticks and burrs after walks, keeping curly coats free of mats before wet weather arrives, and trimming nails as walks get shorter. Do not shave double-coated breeds. Most Cape Cod dogs do well with one professional groom in late September or October, then every 6 to 8 weeks through winter.

Fall on Cape Cod is a great season for dogs. The beaches open back up to them, the trails are quiet, and the air is cool enough for long walks. It is also the season when your dog's coat changes the most. What you do in September and October decides how easy the coat is to manage when the cold, wet months arrive.

1. Get Ahead of the Seasonal Shed

Shorter days trigger a coat change. Your dog drops the lighter summer coat and starts growing a thicker winter undercoat. If the old undercoat is not removed, it packs down against the skin, traps moisture, and makes the new coat grow in dull and tangled.

Double-coated breeds feel this the most: Labradors, Golden Retrievers, Huskies, Bernese Mountain Dogs, German Shepherds, Corgis, and Australian Shepherds. For these dogs, a professional de-shedding treatment is the most useful fall service. It combines a de-shedding shampoo, a high-velocity blow-dry that loosens the undercoat, and a thorough brush-out. You will see far less fur on the couch in the weeks that follow.

Important: do not shave a double-coated dog to "reduce shedding." The undercoat is insulation. Shaving it can leave your dog cold in winter and can damage how the coat grows back.

2. Check for Ticks After Every Walk

Many people assume tick season ends with summer. On Cape Cod, adult deer ticks are active in the fall, especially on mild days in October and November. Tall grass, wooded trails, and dune paths are common places to pick them up.

After walks, run your hands slowly over your dog, paying attention to the ears, neck, armpits, between the toes, and under the tail. Keep your dog on the tick prevention your veterinarian recommends. During every groom at Super Pet, the groomer handles your dog's entire coat and skin up close and will let you know if anything looks unusual.

3. Remove Burrs and Leaves Before They Become Mats

Fall walks bring burrs, seeds, and leaf debris into the coat. In long or curly coats, a single burr can become the center of a tight mat within a few days, especially once the coat gets wet.

Brush your dog two or three times a week during the fall, working all the way to the skin with a slicker brush and then checking with a metal comb. If the comb catches, that section needs more work. Goldendoodles, Labradoodles, Poodles, and other curly breeds need this the most. Our Goldendoodle grooming page explains the coat in more detail.

If mats are already tight against the skin, do not try to cut them out with scissors at home. It is very easy to cut the skin. A groomer can remove them safely. At Super Pet, dematting is charged at $1 per minute and is always discussed with you before it is done.

4. Choose a Fall Length, Not a Summer Length

For breeds that get haircuts, fall is the time to let the coat grow slightly longer. A little extra length keeps your dog warmer on November walks. The trade-off is that a longer coat needs more brushing at home. If you know you will not be able to brush often, a moderate length is better than a long coat that mats.

Tell your groomer how your dog spends the day. A dog who walks the beach every morning needs a different cut than a dog who mostly stays indoors.

5. Trim Nails as Walks Get Shorter

In summer, long walks on pavement and sand wear nails down naturally. In the fall, walks get shorter and softer, and nails grow longer faster than many owners expect. Long nails change the way a dog stands and can make wet floors and icy paths more slippery.

Most dogs need a nail trim every 3 to 4 weeks. If you hear clicking on the floor, it is time.

6. Restore Skin and Coat After Summer

A Cape Cod summer of salt water, sun, and sand leaves many coats dry and brittle. Before indoor heating dries the skin even further, a spa and hydration treatment helps restore moisture. This is especially helpful for dogs that swam often over the summer and for senior dogs with drier skin.

Fall Dog Grooming: Common Questions

Should I shave my dog in the fall?

Not if your dog has a double coat. The undercoat keeps them warm in winter. A de-shedding treatment removes loose undercoat without cutting the coat short. Curly and long-coated breeds can have a trim, usually a little longer than their summer length.

Why does my dog shed so much in the fall?

Shorter days and cooler temperatures trigger a seasonal coat change. Dogs drop the summer coat and grow a denser winter undercoat. The heaviest shedding usually happens in September and October.

Are ticks still a problem on Cape Cod in the fall?

Yes. Adult deer ticks are active in the fall, often through October and November on mild days. Check your dog after every walk in grass, woods, or dunes.

How often should I groom my dog in the fall?

A professional groom every 6 to 8 weeks, plus brushing at home two to three times per week during the shed. Curly-coated breeds that mat easily may need every 4 to 6 weeks.

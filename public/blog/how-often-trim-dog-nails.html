How Often Should You Trim Your Dog's Nails? The Complete Answer

August 6, 2026 · 6 min read · Super Pet Grooming Salon

Most dogs need their nails trimmed every 3 to 6 weeks. Active dogs that walk on pavement frequently may go longer between trims because the surface naturally files down the nails. How Do I Know If My Dog's Nails Are Too Long?

You do not need to measure nails to know when they need attention. There are four clear signals that tell you a trim is overdue:

  • Clicking sound on hard floors. If you can hear your dog walking on tile, hardwood, or laminate, the nails are long enough to make contact with the ground with every step. That is the most reliable indicator that a trim is needed now.
  • Nails visibly extend past the paw pad or curl. When viewed from the side, a properly trimmed nail should not touch the ground when the dog is standing. Nails that curve downward or that you can see extending beyond the paw pad are overdue.
  • Dog walking on the back of its paws. When nails push against the ground, dogs shift their weight backward to compensate. If your dog seems to be toeing oddly or walking with discomfort on hard floors, overgrown nails are a common cause.
  • Nails snagging on carpet or fabric. Long nails, especially dewclaws, catch on rugs, blankets, and upholstery. This is a warning sign that the nails are at risk of tearing, which is painful and sometimes requires veterinary care.

How Often Do Different Dog Types Need Nail Trims?

The right frequency depends on your dog's activity level, age, and lifestyle. Here is a practical breakdown:

  • Active dogs on pavement daily: every 6 to 8 weeks. Concrete and asphalt file nails naturally. Dogs that walk on these surfaces for 30 minutes or more each day wear down their nails faster.
  • House dogs or low-activity dogs: every 3 to 4 weeks. Without pavement to wear nails down, growth accumulates quickly. These dogs may need trims on the shorter end of the range.
  • Senior dogs: every 3 to 4 weeks. Nail growth does not slow with age, and older dogs are often less active. Senior dogs also tend to have thicker, harder nails that are more difficult to trim at home.
  • Puppies: every 2 to 3 weeks while learning. Young dogs benefit from frequent, short nail trim sessions as part of early socialization. Super Pet's Puppy Introduction service ($85 and up) includes nail care and helps puppies learn to tolerate grooming in a calm environment.
  • Dogs with dark nails: same frequency as their activity level would indicate, but professional trimming is more important. Dark nails hide the quick entirely, making safe at-home trimming significantly harder.

On Cape Cod, beach dogs are a category worth noting separately. Sand and soft ground do very little to file nails, despite what many owners assume. A dog that visits the beach three times a week but does not walk on pavement still needs regular professional trims on the same schedule as a house dog.

Does Breed Affect Nail Growth?

Yes, breed plays a meaningful role in both nail growth rate and trim difficulty.

Large breeds like Labrador Retrievers, Golden Retrievers, and German Shepherds have heavier nails that grow at a moderate pace. These dogs are often active enough on pavement to extend the interval between trims, but they still require consistent attention. Left untrimmed, large-breed nails cause significant gait and posture problems because the dog's weight presses down on longer surfaces.

Smaller breeds like Dachshunds, Yorkshire Terriers, Chihuahuas, and Shih Tzus often have faster nail growth relative to their size and spend less time on pavement surfaces that would naturally file the nails. Many small-breed owners find that a 3 to 4 week trim schedule is the only way to stay ahead of the growth.

Cape Cod beach dogs, regardless of breed, tend to have sandier, softer nails from salt water and sand exposure. While this makes trimming marginally easier, it does not reduce the frequency needed. A professional trim every 4 to 6 weeks remains the standard.

What Happens If Nails Are Left Too Long?

Overgrown nails create a chain of problems that go beyond cosmetics:

  • Chronic pain while walking. Every step pushes the nail back into the nail bed. Dogs adapt to this discomfort, which is why owners often do not notice it until the nails are significantly overgrown.
  • Posture and joint problems. Altered weight distribution from long nails changes how a dog stands and moves. Over months and years, this puts abnormal stress on the toes, ankles, and even the hips and knees.
  • Broken nails. An overgrown nail is more likely to catch on a surface and tear partially or fully. A torn nail is one of the most painful injuries a dog can experience outside of a vet office, and it often requires professional treatment to manage the bleeding and risk of infection.
  • Infection risk. Nails that grow in a curve can eventually press into the paw pad, creating a wound that is prone to infection. This is more common in senior dogs whose nails may be neglected for longer periods.

Professional vs. At-Home Nail Trim

At-home nail trimming is possible and, for some dogs and owners, perfectly manageable. The right tools are a sharp pair of dog nail clippers (scissor-style or guillotine-style) or a rotary grinder. The key risk is cutting the quick, the blood vessel that runs inside the nail.

For dogs with white or light-colored nails, the quick is visible as a pink area, making it relatively easy to stay safely short of it. For dogs with dark or black nails, the quick is completely hidden. Cutting into it is painful for the dog and causes immediate bleeding. Many owners understandably prefer to have a professional groomer handle dark-nailed dogs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I trim my dog's nails?

Most dogs need nail trims every 3 to 6 weeks. Dogs that are very active on pavement may need less frequent trims. Senior dogs and house dogs typically need trims every 3 to 4 weeks. The clearest sign it is time for a trim is when you can hear the nails clicking on hard floors.

Can I trim my dog's nails at home?

Yes, but it requires the right tools (sharp dog clippers or a grinder) and care to avoid cutting the quick. Dogs with dark nails are harder to trim safely at home because the quick is not visible. Many dog owners prefer professional nail trims for safety and convenience. Super Pet in East Falmouth charges $20 per trim. Call (508) 524-3489 to book.

What is the quick, and why does it matter?

The quick is the blood vessel inside the nail. Cutting into it causes bleeding and pain. White or light-colored nails show the quick as a pink area, making it easy to avoid. Dark nails hide the quick entirely, which is why professional trimming is recommended for dogs with dark nails.

Winter Dog Grooming on Cape Cod: Coat, Paw and Skin Care Guide

September 23, 2026 · 6 min read · Super Pet Grooming Salon

Quick answer: Keep grooming every 6 to 8 weeks in winter, but leave the coat a little longer. Wipe paws after walks on salted roads, keep the hair between the pads short, brush under sweaters and harnesses to prevent mats, fight dry skin from indoor heat with moisturizing products, and keep nails short for grip on ice.

A Cape Cod winter is not the harshest in New England, but it has its own challenges: wet nor'easters, salt spray along the shore, treated roads and parking lots, and months of dry indoor heat. Each one affects your dog's coat, paws, or skin. Here is how to handle all of them.

Coat: Longer, but Not Neglected

Many owners let the coat grow all winter to keep their dog warm. A little extra length is a good idea. Skipping grooming entirely is not. Wet snow and rain soak into the coat, and when it dries in a house with the heat on, the hair tangles into mats that hold moisture against the skin.

Ask your groomer for a winter length: slightly longer on the body, with the feet, face, and sanitary areas kept tidy. Keep the regular 6 to 8 week schedule for a bath and full groom, and every 4 to 6 weeks for curly coats like Goldendoodles.

Double-coated breeds still should not be shaved. Their undercoat is natural insulation.

Sweaters and Harnesses Cause Hidden Mats

Dog sweaters and coats are helpful for small, short-haired, and senior dogs. But fabric rubbing against the fur all day creates friction mats, usually in the armpits, chest, and behind the legs. The same happens under harness straps.

Take the sweater off at least once a day and brush those areas. If you find mats that are tight to the skin, leave them for a groomer. At Super Pet, dematting is charged at $1 per minute and always discussed with you first. See our guide to matted dog fur for more.

Paws: Salt, Ice Melt, and Ice Balls

Roads, sidewalks, and parking lots in Falmouth and across the Cape are treated with salt and ice melt after storms. These products can dry and crack paw pads, and dogs often lick them off their feet afterward.

  • Wipe paws with a damp cloth after every walk on treated surfaces, then dry between the toes.
  • Apply a pet-safe paw balm before walks to create a protective layer.
  • Keep the hair between the paw pads trimmed short. Long hair there collects snow, which freezes into painful ice balls.
  • Consider dog boots for long walks on heavily salted streets.

A paw tidy is included in every full groom, and it is available on its own with a nail trim and paw care appointment from $20.

Nails: Short Nails Mean Better Grip

Winter walks are shorter and softer, so nails wear down less. Long nails push the toes up and reduce contact between the pads and the ground, which makes icy paths and wet kitchen floors more slippery. This matters most for senior dogs and large breeds. Trim every 3 to 4 weeks through the winter.

Skin: Fighting Dry Indoor Heat

Heated homes can have very low humidity in January and February. Many dogs develop dry, flaky skin and a dull coat. To help:

  • Brush regularly to spread the coat's natural oils.
  • Use a moisturizing shampoo and conditioner. Avoid frequent bathing with harsh products at home.
  • Book a spa and hydration treatment (from $50) once or twice during the winter.
  • A humidifier in the room where your dog sleeps helps both of you.

If flaking is severe, or comes with redness, hair loss, or constant scratching, see your veterinarian. It may not be simple winter dryness.

Bathing in Winter: Dry Completely

Winter baths are safe as long as the coat is fully dry before your dog goes outside. A damp undercoat loses its insulation. At Super Pet, every dog is hand-dried all the way to the skin, never cage dried, so your dog leaves warm and dry even in January.

Winter Dog Grooming: Common Questions

Should I stop grooming my dog in the winter?

No. Skipping winter grooming is a common cause of severe matting. Keep a groom every 6 to 8 weeks and ask for a slightly longer length for warmth.

How do I protect my dog's paws from road salt?

Wipe paws after walks on treated surfaces, dry between the toes, use a pet-safe paw balm, and keep the hair between the pads trimmed short.

Is it safe to bathe a dog in winter?

Yes, as long as the dog is completely dry before going outside. At Super Pet, every dog is hand-dried to the skin before pickup.

Why is my dog's skin dry and flaky in winter?

Indoor heating lowers humidity. Regular brushing, moisturizing products, and a spa and hydration treatment help. See your veterinarian if it is severe.

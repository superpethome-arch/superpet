Full Groom vs. Bath Only: Which Service Does Your Dog Actually Need?

June 27, 2026 · 4 min read · Super Pet Grooming Salon

When it comes time to book a grooming appointment, one of the most common questions we hear at Super Pet Grooming Salon is: "Should I book a bath or a full groom?" It seems like a simple question, but the answer depends on your dog's breed, coat type, current condition, and how long it has been since their last professional grooming session. Understanding the difference between the two services helps you make a better choice for your dog and your budget.

What Is Included in a Bath Only Service?

A bath service focuses on cleaning your dog thoroughly without a haircut. At Super Pet, a bath appointment typically includes a full shampoo and conditioning treatment using products suited to your dog's skin and coat, a blow-dry, brushing to remove loose fur and tangles, ear cleaning, and a spritz of dog-safe fragrance to leave your pup smelling fresh.

A bath is a great option for short-coated breeds like Beagles, Boxers, and Labrador Retrievers, whose coats do not need trimming but still benefit from professional cleaning and drying. It is also a good maintenance service between full groom appointments for dogs with longer coats, helping to keep the coat in good condition without a full cut.

If your dog just had a full groom a few weeks ago and you want to freshen them up before a family gathering or a trip to the vet, a bath appointment is often all you need. You can also add a nail trim or de-shedding treatment to any bath visit for an extra boost.

What Is Included in a Full Groom?

A full groom includes everything in a bath service, plus a haircut and trim. Your groomer will clip or scissor the coat to your requested style, trim around the face, paws, ears, and tail, and tidy up any areas that are prone to matting or overgrowth. Sanitary trimming around the rear is also part of a standard full groom.

Full grooms are essential for breeds with continuously growing coats, such as Poodles, Bichon Frises, Shih Tzus, Goldendoodles, and Schnauzers. Without regular haircuts, these breeds develop matting that can be uncomfortable and even painful. A full groom keeps their coat at a manageable length and their skin healthy underneath.

Most dogs with longer or curly coats should have a full groom every six to eight weeks. You can read more about timing in our guide on how often you should groom your dog.

How to Decide Which Service Your Dog Needs

The simplest way to decide is to look at your dog's coat type. If your dog has a coat that grows continuously, a full groom is the right choice for most visits. If your dog has a short, wash-and-wear coat, a bath service is usually sufficient.

Also consider how long it has been since your dog's last professional groom. If it has been several months and there is noticeable growth, matting, or tangling, a full groom will address all of those issues in one appointment. If the coat is in good shape and you just want to get rid of dirt and odor, a bath may be all your dog needs.

When in doubt, give us a call at (508) 524-3489 and describe your dog's breed and coat condition. We can help you choose the right service before you even walk through the door.

Can You Mix and Match Services?

Absolutely. At Super Pet, our services are designed to work together. You can book a bath and add on a nail trim, a de-shedding treatment, or our spa package. You can also schedule a full groom one visit and a bath-only visit in between, which works well for dogs whose coats grow quickly and need freshening up more often than a full cut.

For dogs who experience anxiety during grooming, breaking the process into smaller visits can also help. A bath-only appointment is shorter and lower-stress, making it a useful step for dogs who are still getting comfortable with the grooming environment.

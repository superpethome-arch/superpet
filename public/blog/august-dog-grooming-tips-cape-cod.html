August Dog Grooming on Cape Cod: 7 Tips to Keep Your Dog Cool and Clean

August 6, 2026 · 7 min read · Super Pet Grooming Salon

August is the hottest and busiest month on Cape Cod. With beach trips, outdoor activities, and high humidity all peaking at once, your dog's coat and skin need extra attention this time of year. Here are seven practical tips from Super Pet Grooming Salon in East Falmouth to keep your dog comfortable through the final weeks of summer. For appointments, call (508) 524-3489 or book online.

Tip 1: Schedule a Groom Before the Rush Ends

August is the peak season for grooming appointments on Cape Cod. Seasonal residents, vacationers, and full-time locals are all competing for the same slots during this window. If your dog is overdue for a bath and full groom, do not wait until the last week of the month.

Super Pet Grooming Salon in East Falmouth stays consistently booked in August. Booking one to two weeks in advance gives you the best chance of landing a time that works for your schedule. For dogs with longer coats that tangle easily in humidity, an early-August groom before the hottest stretch is especially valuable.

A full groom at Super Pet starts at $75 and includes a bath, blow-dry, haircut, and nail trim. Sabina, the lead certified groomer, has over 10 years of experience with breeds of all types, from small terriers to large retrievers. Call (508) 524-3489 to check current availability.

Tip 2: Rinse After Every Beach Trip

Salt water and sand are the two biggest coat stressors for Cape Cod dogs in August. Salt water dries out the skin and coat when it is left to air dry, and sand packs into the fur, especially around the paws, belly, and behind the ears. These areas are the hardest to clean and the most prone to irritation when left uncleaned after a beach visit.

A quick rinse with fresh water immediately after leaving the beach makes a real difference. Focus on the paws, belly, groin area, and the insides of the ears. A garden hose works, but the rinse is necessarily quick and often incomplete.

A professional bath at Super Pet is a more thorough solution, especially for dogs that visit the beach multiple times per week. The salon uses professional-grade shampoos that break down salt residue and lift sand from the undercoat, areas that a surface rinse simply does not reach. The $75-plus bath and groom service includes a full blow-dry and brush-out that restores the coat to a clean, comfortable state.

Tip 3: Watch for Hot Spots and Skin Irritation

Hot spots are localized areas of inflamed, infected skin that dogs scratch or chew. They are particularly common in August on Cape Cod because the combination of high humidity, salt water exposure, and heat creates ideal conditions for skin bacteria to thrive. Dogs with thicker or longer coats are more susceptible, but any breed can develop hot spots during a wet, warm month.

Early signs include redness, excessive licking or chewing at a specific area, and wet or matted fur in one location. If you notice any of these, schedule a grooming appointment promptly. Sabina can assess the coat condition and recommend the appropriate shampoo or treatment. Left unaddressed, hot spots spread quickly and may require veterinary attention.

Using the right shampoo for your dog's skin type matters. Super Pet carries professional grooming products appropriate for sensitive or irritated skin. Mention any skin concerns when booking your appointment.

Tip 4: Keep Nails Trimmed for Sandy Terrain

Beach sand feels soft underfoot, but it has a surprising effect on dog nails. Sand packs tightly around overgrown nails and into the grooves between the paw pads, creating discomfort and sometimes inflammation. Dogs with longer nails are also more likely to catch them on driftwood, rocks, and boat dock planks, all common obstacles along the Cape Cod shoreline.

The standard recommendation is a nail trim every four to six weeks. If you can hear your dog's nails clicking on hardwood or tile, they are overdue. Super Pet offers standalone nail trims starting at $20, and nail trims can be added to any grooming appointment.

On National Dog Day, Wednesday August 26, 2026 from 1:00 PM to 3:00 PM, Super Pet is offering a free professional nail trim to every dog. This is the perfect opportunity to get your dog's nails trimmed at no cost. RSVP at superpethome.com/event to reserve your spot.

Tip 5: De-Shed Before Labor Day

Heavy shedding coats trap heat. Dogs like Labrador Retrievers, German Shepherds, Golden Retrievers, and Bernese Mountain Dogs carry a thick undercoat that continues to shed through August. That loose undercoat does not fall out on its own, it accumulates in the coat and traps warm air against the skin.

A professional de-shedding treatment removes the bulk of that loose undercoat with a targeted process that goes beyond a standard brush-out. At Super Pet, de-shedding is a $30 add-on to any grooming appointment. For heavy-shedding breeds, scheduling a de-shedding session before Labor Day weekend means your dog enters September lighter, cooler, and with less loose fur around your home.

De-shedding is not a substitute for brushing between appointments. Aim to brush heavy-shedding breeds at least two to three times per week in August.

Tip 6: Brush More Frequently in August

Heat, humidity, and outdoor activity all accelerate matting in August. This is especially true for doodle breeds, including Goldendoodles, Labradoodles, and Bernedoodles, whose curly or wavy coats are prone to tangling even under normal conditions. In August conditions on Cape Cod, a dog that skips a week of brushing can develop tight mats behind the ears, in the armpits, and along the belly in a short amount of time.

Brushing two to three times per week is the minimum for most medium and long-coated breeds in August. Use a slicker brush to work through the surface coat, followed by a comb to check the undercoat for developing mats. Pay extra attention to the collar area, where friction and moisture create mats faster than anywhere else.

If you discover a mat that is too tight to work through without pulling, do not cut it with scissors at home. Bring the dog in to Super Pet for a dematting session at $1 per minute, which is far safer than risking a cut to the skin.

Tip 7: Plan Your Post-Summer Groom

The last week of August and the first week of September is the ideal time to schedule a coat refresh after beach season. A full groom at this point removes the accumulated sun damage, salt residue, and tangles that summer leaves behind, and sets the coat up for the transition to fall.

This appointment tends to book up quickly as Labor Day approaches. Schedule your early-September groom in advance while you are booking any remaining August appointments. Sabina and the Super Pet team will assess the coat condition and recommend whether a standard groom, a de-shedding session, or a dematting treatment is the right approach for your dog's specific needs after a full summer on Cape Cod.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I groom my dog in August on Cape Cod?

During August, most dogs with medium to long coats benefit from a professional groom every 4 to 6 weeks. Dogs that visit the beach frequently may need bathing more often between scheduled grooms. Super Pet Grooming Salon in East Falmouth offers appointments Monday through Friday, 9am to 5pm. Call (508) 524-3489 to check availability.

Is it safe to trim my dog's coat short in August heat?

For many breeds, a shorter trim helps significantly in August heat. However, some double-coated breeds like Huskies and Bernese Mountain Dogs should not be shaved, as their coat provides natural insulation against both heat and sun. Ask Sabina at Super Pet for a breed-appropriate recommendation before making a trim decision.

How do I rinse salt water off my dog after the beach?

Use fresh water immediately after leaving the beach. Focus on the paws, belly, and behind the ears where salt and sand accumulate most. A full bath with professional shampoo at Super Pet removes deep residue that a garden hose rinse can miss, particularly in the undercoat and around the ears.

Can Super Pet handle emergency grooming if my dog's coat mats badly in August?

Yes. Super Pet offers dematting services at $1 per minute for severe cases. It is always better to book a professional dematting session than to attempt to cut mats at home, which risks cutting the skin. Call (508) 524-3489 to schedule a dematting appointment.
